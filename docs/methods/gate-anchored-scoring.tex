\title{Gate-Anchored Scoring for the Topos Evaluation Lattice}
\author{Topos maintainers}
\date{October 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Topos grades each source file on four pillars (SIMPLE, COMPOSABLE, SECURE,
NAVIGABLE). Each pillar has a binary verdict, decided by gates on raw metrics,
and a continuous score in $[0,100]$. Until now the score was the minimum over
linear quality curves whose caps were unrelated to the gates and which also
included advisory metrics, so score and verdict routinely disagreed. We replace
it with a four-layer construction: (i)~gate-anchored desirability functions
with value exactly $\tau = 1/2$ at each gate; (ii)~Gödel ($\min$) aggregation of
gates, which we show is the unique t-norm commuting with every $\alpha$-cut;
(iii)~codebase-relative advisory percentiles shrunk toward a per-language prior,
which we identify as a Dirichlet-process posterior mean and calibrate by a
normal--normal variance ratio; and (iv)~a banded combination that lets
advisories move a score only within the half chosen by the gates. The score
vector then lives in a product of Gödel chains, of which the existing
16-element verdict lattice is the $\tau$-cut. On the leaderboard corpus the
number of passing files that score below $50$ falls from $4{,}757$ to $8$
(SIMPLE, $30{,}686$ files) and from $302$ to $0$ (COMPOSABLE, $1{,}061$ files).
\end{abstract}

\section{Notation}

\begin{itemize}
  \item $\mathcal P = \{\mathrm{SIMPLE}, \mathrm{COMPOSABLE}, \mathrm{SECURE},
        \mathrm{NAVIGABLE}\}$ is the set of pillars; $k$ ranges over $\mathcal P$
        only in Section~\ref{sec:lattice}, where it cannot be confused with the
        shrinkage constant.
  \item For a file $f$ and metric $m$, $v_m(f) \in [0,\infty)$ is the raw
        reading.
  \item A \emph{gated} metric $m$ of pillar $p$ has a global gate $g_m$; the
        gate predicate $\pi_m(v) \in \{0,1\}$ is $\ind{v \le g_m}$,
        $\ind{v \in [\ell_m, u_m]}$ or $\ind{v = 0}$ according to its kind.
        Pillar $p$ passes iff every gate of $p$ passes.
  \item $\tau = 1/2$ is the anchor. Scores are reported on $[0,100]$ as
        $100 \cdot S$.
  \item $\ind{\varphi} \in \{0,1\}$ is the Iverson bracket.
  \item $T$ denotes a t-norm: a commutative, associative binary operation on
        $[0,1]$, nondecreasing in each argument, with $T(x,1) = x$.
\end{itemize}

\section{The previous scoring and its failure}

Each pillar $p$ had a score map $\Phi_p$ that sent every \emph{scored} metric
(gated or advisory) through a linear quality curve $q_m(v) = \max(0, 1 - v/c_m)$
with a cap $c_m$ unrelated to $g_m$ (for example cyclomatic cap $40$ against an
advisory reference of $15$; max-function-complexity cap $20$ against gate $10$),
and reported $\min_m q_m$. The verdict, in contrast, used only gated metrics.
Two defects follow.

\paragraph{Score--verdict contradiction.} Because the minimum ranges over
advisory metrics and the caps are not anchored to gates, a passing file can
score arbitrarily low. On the leaderboard corpus:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
pillar & files scored & pass with score $< 50$ \\
\midrule
SIMPLE (leaderboard) & 30{,}686 & 4{,}757 \\
COMPOSABLE (fresh run) & 1{,}061 & 302 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{COMPOSABLE artifacts.} The instability $I = C_e/(C_a + C_e)$ was
scored against a fixed band $[0.3, 0.7]$ (issue \#351):
\begin{itemize}
  \item files at $I = 0$ or $I = 1$, the two commonest values, scored $0\%$;
  \item main-sequence distance $D = |A + I - 1|$ only contributed when the
        abstractness $A > 0$, which held for $62$ of $1{,}061$ files;
  \item $41\%$ of files have $C_a + C_e < 2$ and received a fabricated
        $I = 0.5$;
  \item with $n = C_a + C_e$, attainable values are $\{j/n\}$, so the in-band
        rate is an artifact of the denominator: $31\%, 44\%, 16\%, 46\%$ for
        $n = 2, 3, 4, 5$.
\end{itemize}
The leaderboard calibration report described ``an extremely binary
distribution for modularity'' and used it to justify a COMPOSABLE score floor of
$0.80$. That distribution is the band artifact above, not a property of the
code.

\section{Gate-anchored desirability}

Following Derringer and Suich~\cite{derringer1980}, each gated metric is mapped
to a desirability $d \in [0,1]$; we additionally anchor it so that
$d = \tau$ exactly at the gate.

\begin{definition}[Lower-is-better]
For a gate $g > 0$,
\[
d_g(v) =
\begin{cases}
1 - \dfrac{v}{2g}, & 0 \le v \le g,\\[6pt]
\dfrac{2g - v}{2g}, & g < v < 2g,\\[6pt]
0, & v \ge 2g.
\end{cases}
\]
This applies to max function complexity, $\mathrm{fan\_out}$ and max function
divergence, each with $g = 10$.
\end{definition}

The two linear pieces coincide ($d = 1 - v/(2g)$ on $[0, 2g]$); we keep the
split to make the anchor visible. The map is continuous and nonincreasing, with
$d_g(0) = 1$, $d_g(g) = \tfrac12$ and $d_g(2g) = 0$.

\begin{definition}[Band]
For a band $[\ell, u] \subset (0,1)$ with ideal $c \in (\ell, u)$ on the domain
$[0,1]$, let $d_{\ell,c,u}$ be the piecewise-linear function through the points
$(0,0)$, $(\ell, \tfrac12)$, $(c, 1)$, $(u, \tfrac12)$, $(1, 0)$:
\[
d_{\ell,c,u}(v) =
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{v}{2\ell}, & 0 \le v \le \ell,\\[6pt]
\dfrac12 + \dfrac{v - \ell}{2(c - \ell)}, & \ell < v \le c,\\[6pt]
\dfrac12 + \dfrac{u - v}{2(u - c)}, & c < v \le u,\\[6pt]
\dfrac{1 - v}{2(1 - u)}, & u < v \le 1.
\end{cases}
\]
AST entropy uses $(\ell, c, u) = (0.2, 0.5, 0.8)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Zero tolerance]
For count metrics (dangerous calls, taint flows),
$d_0(v) = 1$ if $v = 0$ and $d_0(v) = \tfrac12 e^{-v/3}$ if $v > 0$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Exemption clamp]
If a gate is exempt for file $f$ (for example the entropy entrypoint
exemption), its desirability is replaced by $\max(d, \tau)$, and its gate
predicate is $1$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Anchoring]\label{lem:anchor}
For every gated metric and every reading $v$, $d(v) \ge \tau \iff \pi(v) = 1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Lower-is-better.} On $[0,g]$, $d_g(v) = 1 - v/(2g) \ge 1 - g/(2g) = \tfrac12$.
For $v \in (g, 2g)$, $d_g(v) = (2g - v)/(2g) < g/(2g) = \tfrac12$. For
$v \ge 2g$, $d_g(v) = 0 < \tfrac12$. Hence $d_g(v) \ge \tfrac12 \iff v \le g$.

\emph{Band.} On $[0,\ell)$, $d = v/(2\ell) < \tfrac12$; at $v = \ell$, $d = \tfrac12$.
On $(\ell, c]$, $d = \tfrac12 + (v-\ell)/(2(c-\ell)) > \tfrac12$. On $(c, u]$,
$d = \tfrac12 + (u - v)/(2(u-c)) \ge \tfrac12$ with equality at $v = u$. On
$(u, 1]$, $d = (1-v)/(2(1-u)) < (1-u)/(2(1-u)) = \tfrac12$. Hence
$d \ge \tfrac12 \iff v \in [\ell, u]$.

\emph{Zero tolerance.} $d_0(0) = 1 \ge \tfrac12$, and for $v > 0$,
$d_0(v) = \tfrac12 e^{-v/3} < \tfrac12$.

\emph{Exemption.} The gate passes by definition and $\max(d, \tau) \ge \tau$.
\end{proof}

\section{Aggregation within a pillar}

The gate score of pillar $p$ with gated metrics $m_1, \dots, m_r$ is
\[
G_p = \min_{i} d_{m_i}(v_{m_i}).
\]

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:gate}
Pillar $p$ passes iff $G_p \ge \tau$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
$\min_i d_i \ge \tau \iff \forall i,\ d_i \ge \tau \iff \forall i,\ \pi_i = 1$
by Lemma~\ref{lem:anchor}.
\end{proof}

Why $\min$ rather than a product or a mean? The requirement is that the
continuous aggregate can be read at a threshold exactly as the crisp
conjunction is.

\begin{proposition}[Uniqueness of the Gödel t-norm]\label{prop:min}
Let $T$ be a t-norm. The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item for all $\alpha, x, y \in [0,1]$:
        $T(x,y) \ge \alpha \iff (x \ge \alpha \text{ and } y \ge \alpha)$;
  \item $T = \min$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(2)$\Rightarrow$(1): $\min(x,y) \ge \alpha$ iff both arguments are $\ge \alpha$.

(1)$\Rightarrow$(2): For any t-norm, monotonicity and the unit give
$T(x,y) \le T(x,1) = x$ and, by commutativity, $T(x,y) \le y$; so
$T(x,y) \le \min(x,y)$. Now apply (1) with $\alpha = \min(x,y)$: both
$x \ge \alpha$ and $y \ge \alpha$ hold, so $T(x,y) \ge \min(x,y)$. Hence
$T = \min$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[A single threshold]
If (1) is required only at the one level $\alpha = \tau$, the condition is
weaker: since $T \le \min$, it is equivalent to $T(x,y) \ge \tau$ whenever
$x, y \ge \tau$, which by monotonicity is equivalent to $T(\tau, \tau) = \tau$,
i.e.\ $\tau$ is an idempotent of $T$. Ordinal sums with a summand boundary at
$\tau$ satisfy it without being $\min$. We require (1) at every $\alpha$ because
the score is also read at levels other than $\tau$ (for example when comparing
two passing files, or reading the medal scores of Section~\ref{sec:lattice}),
and $\min$ is the only t-norm all of whose elements are idempotent.
\end{remark}

\begin{example}[Common aggregators fail at $\alpha = \tau$]
\leavevmode
\begin{itemize}
  \item Product: $x = y = 0.6$ both pass, but $xy = 0.36 < 0.5$.
  \item Łukasiewicz $T_L(x,y) = \max(0, x + y - 1)$: $T_L(0.6, 0.6) = 0.2 < 0.5$.
  \item Geometric mean $\sqrt{xy}$ (not a t-norm): $x = 0.9$, $y = 0.4$ gives
        $0.6 \ge 0.5$ although the second gate fails. The arithmetic mean fails
        the same way.
\end{itemize}
Product and Łukasiewicz are Archimedean, so $T(\tau,\tau) < \tau$ for every
$\tau \in (0,1)$ and they fail at every threshold.
\end{example}

The Gödel t-norm is the conjunction of Gödel fuzzy logic~\cite{hajek1998};
$\min$ as a ``pessimistic'' multi-criteria aggregator also appears in
Yager's OWA family~\cite{yager1988} as its extreme case.

\section{Codebase-relative advisory metrics}\label{sec:advisory}

Advisory metrics never gate. Fixed caps for them imported an implicit global
threshold that the calibration never justified. Instead we read each advisory
metric as a percentile of the codebase it lives in, stabilized by a
per-language prior. Percentile-based benchmarking of code metrics is standard
practice~\cite{alves2010,alves2011,oliveira2014,foucault2014}; context matters
across projects and languages~\cite{zhang2013,gil2016}.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
pillar & advisory metric & direction \\
\midrule
SIMPLE & \texttt{cfg.cyclomatic} & higher is worse \\
SIMPLE & \texttt{cfg.nesting\_depth} & higher is worse \\
SIMPLE & \texttt{cfg.essential} & higher is worse \\
COMPOSABLE & \texttt{mdg.fan\_in} & higher is worse \\
COMPOSABLE & \texttt{mdg.instability} (only if $C_a + C_e \ge 2$) & two-sided \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Main-sequence distance and the instability band are removed from scoring.

\subsection{Mid-rank empirical CDF}

\begin{definition}
For a finite sample $x_1, \dots, x_n$ and $x \in \R$,
\[
F_n^{\mathrm{mid}}(x) = \frac{\#\{j : x_j < x\} + \tfrac12 \#\{j : x_j = x\}}{n}
= \tfrac12\bigl(F_n(x^-) + F_n(x)\bigr),
\]
where $F_n$ is the right-continuous ECDF. (This is the mid-distribution
function; compare the plotting-position conventions surveyed by Hyndman and
Fan~\cite{hyndman1996}.)
\end{definition}

Ties are the norm: $46$--$88\%$ of files have zero divergence and $52$--$63\%$
have zero fan-in or fan-out, depending on ecosystem. The right-continuous ECDF
would assign every zero the percentile $p_0$; the left limit would assign $0$.
The mid-rank assigns $p_0/2$, the expected percentile under random tie-breaking,
and is symmetric: $F^{\mathrm{mid}}$ of a sample reflected about any point
equals one minus the original.

\subsection{Partial pooling}

For a file with reading $x$ in a codebase whose readings of the same metric are
$x_1, \dots, x_n$ (the file itself included), set
\[
\Ft(x) = w\,\Floc(x) + (1 - w)\,\Flang(x), \qquad w = \frac{n}{n + k},
\]
where $\Floc$ is the mid-rank ECDF of the codebase, $\Flang$ is the mid-rank
CDF of a per-language prior table, and $k > 0$ is a per-metric constant.
With no population ($n = 0$) we get $\Ft = \Flang$.

\begin{proposition}[Dirichlet-process posterior mean]\label{prop:dp}
Let $P \sim \mathrm{DP}(k, \Flang)$ be a Dirichlet process with concentration
$k$ and base distribution $\Flang$~\cite{ferguson1973}, and let
$x_1, \dots, x_n \mid P \stackrel{\text{iid}}{\sim} P$. Then for every $x$,
\[
\mathbb E\bigl[\tfrac12\bigl(P(-\infty, x) + P(-\infty, x]\bigr) \bigm| x_{1:n}\bigr]
= \frac{n}{n+k}\,\Floc(x) + \frac{k}{n+k}\,\Flang(x),
\]
with $\Floc$ and $\Flang$ in their mid-rank forms.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By conjugacy~\cite{ferguson1973}, $P \mid x_{1:n} \sim \mathrm{DP}\bigl(k + n,
\bar G\bigr)$ with
$\bar G = \frac{k}{k+n}\Flang + \frac{1}{k+n}\sum_{j} \delta_{x_j}$. For a
Dirichlet process $\mathrm{DP}(c, H)$ and any measurable set $B$,
$P(B) \sim \mathrm{Beta}(cH(B), c(1 - H(B)))$, so $\mathbb E[P(B)] = H(B)$.
Taking $B = (-\infty, x]$ and $B = (-\infty, x)$,
\[
\mathbb E[P(-\infty, x] \mid x_{1:n}] = \tfrac{k}{k+n}\Flang(x) + \tfrac{n}{k+n} F_n(x),
\quad
\mathbb E[P(-\infty, x) \mid x_{1:n}] = \tfrac{k}{k+n}\Flang(x^-) + \tfrac{n}{k+n} F_n(x^-).
\]
Averaging the two and using linearity of expectation gives the claim, since
$\tfrac12(F_n(x^-) + F_n(x)) = \Floc(x)$ and likewise for $\Flang$.
\end{proof}

So $\Ft$ is the Bayes estimate, under squared error, of the codebase's own
mid-distribution function, with the language prior acting as $k$ pseudo-files.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Choosing $k$}{Choosing k}: normal--normal shrinkage}

The DP view says what $\Ft$ is but not how large $k$ should be. We calibrate
$k$ by matching the weight a hierarchical location model would place on a
codebase's own data. Let $y_{ij} = \log(1 + x_{ij})$ for file $j$ of package
$i$ within one language, and
\[
y_{ij} = \theta_i + e_{ij}, \quad e_{ij} \sim N(0, \sigma_w^2), \qquad
\theta_i \sim N(\mu, \sigma_b^2),
\]
independently. (The design notes write $\tau^2$ for $\sigma_b^2$; we avoid that
symbol because $\tau$ is the anchor.)

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:shrink}
Given $n$ files of package $i$ with mean $\bar y_i$,
\[
\mathbb E[\theta_i \mid y_{i\cdot}] = w\,\bar y_i + (1-w)\,\mu,
\qquad w = \frac{n}{n + k}, \qquad k = \frac{\sigma_w^2}{\sigma_b^2}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\bar y_i \mid \theta_i \sim N(\theta_i, \sigma_w^2/n)$ is sufficient for
$\theta_i$. The normal prior is conjugate, and the posterior mean is the
precision-weighted average
\[
\frac{(n/\sigma_w^2)\,\bar y_i + (1/\sigma_b^2)\,\mu}{n/\sigma_w^2 + 1/\sigma_b^2}
= \frac{n}{n + \sigma_w^2/\sigma_b^2}\,\bar y_i
+ \frac{\sigma_w^2/\sigma_b^2}{n + \sigma_w^2/\sigma_b^2}\,\mu. \qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

This is the James--Stein / empirical-Bayes weight of Efron and
Morris~\cite{efron1975}. Using the same $k$ as the DP concentration means the
codebase ECDF receives the weight that a package's own mean would receive in
the location model. This is a matching choice, not a theorem about CDFs; the
next subsection shows it has the right form for CDF estimation as well.

\paragraph{Method-of-moments estimator.} Let packages $i = 1, \dots, M$ have
$n_i \ge 2$ files, means $\bar y_i$ and within-package sample variances $s_i^2$.
Estimate
\[
\hat\sigma_w^2 = \frac{\sum_i (n_i - 1) s_i^2}{\sum_i (n_i - 1)}, \qquad
\hat\sigma_b^2 = \max\Bigl(0,\; \widehat{\mathrm{Var}}(\bar y_i)
- \frac{1}{M}\sum_i \frac{\hat\sigma_w^2}{n_i}\Bigr),
\]
where $\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}(\bar y_i)$ is the sample variance of the package
means. The subtracted term is the noise correction: since
$\mathrm{Var}(\bar y_i) = \sigma_b^2 + \sigma_w^2/n_i$, the raw spread of
package means overstates between-package variance by the average sampling
variance of a mean. (In the balanced case this coincides with the one-way
random-effects ANOVA estimator $(\mathrm{MSB} - \mathrm{MSW})/n$.) Then
$\hat k = \hat\sigma_w^2/\hat\sigma_b^2$, with $\hat k = \infty$ (prior only)
if $\hat\sigma_b^2 = 0$.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lr@{\qquad}lr}
\toprule
metric & $\hat k$ & metric & $\hat k$ \\
\midrule
max function complexity & 3.7 & longest path & 3.9 \\
cyclomatic & 3.8 & entropy & 4.6 \\
nesting depth & 3.8 & divergence & 6.4 \\
essential & 3.1 & & \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

With $k \approx 4$, a 10-file repository receives $w \approx 0.71$ and a 50-file
repository $w \approx 0.93$.

\subsection{Small-sample behavior}

At a fixed $x$ with true codebase CDF value $p = F(x)$, the ECDF is unbiased with
standard error $\sqrt{p(1-p)/n}$. At the flag level $p = 0.9$ and $n = 10$ this
is $0.095$: an unpooled local percentile would wander across the flag boundary
by chance. Treating $\Flang$ as fixed,
\[
\mathrm{Var}\bigl(\Ft(x)\bigr) = w^2 \frac{p(1-p)}{n}, \qquad
\mathrm{Bias}\bigl(\Ft(x)\bigr) = (1 - w)\bigl(\Flang(x) - p\bigr).
\]
With $n = 10$, $k = 4$ the standard deviation drops to $0.068$. Write
$\Delta = \Flang(x) - p$. Minimizing the mean squared error
$w^2 p(1-p)/n + (1-w)^2 \Delta^2$ over $w$ gives
\[
w^\star = \frac{\Delta^2}{\Delta^2 + p(1-p)/n} = \frac{n}{n + p(1-p)/\Delta^2},
\]
the same $n/(n+k)$ form with $k^\star = p(1-p)/\Delta^2$: sampling noise over
squared between-codebase discrepancy, the CDF analog of
$\sigma_w^2/\sigma_b^2$. As $n \to \infty$, $w \to 1$ and $\Ft$ converges to the
codebase's own distribution.

\subsection{Quality, pillar advisory score, and flags}

\begin{definition}
For an advisory reading at value $x$, let
$\Ft^{<}(x) = w\,\Pr_{\mathrm{loc}}(X < x) + (1-w)\,\Pr_{\mathrm{lang}}(X < x)$
be the pooled \emph{strict} lower CDF (the share of the pooled population
strictly better than $x$). The advisory quality is
\[
a = \begin{cases}
1 - \Ft^{<}(x), & \text{higher is worse},\\
1 - 2\,\bigl|\Ft(x) - \tfrac12\bigr|, & \text{two-sided},
\end{cases}
\]
and the pillar advisory score is the geometric mean
$A_p = \bigl(\prod_{m} \max(a_m, \varepsilon)\bigr)^{1/|M_p|}$ over the
applicable advisory metrics $M_p$ of $p$, with $A_p = 1$ if $M_p = \emptyset$ and
$\varepsilon = 10^{-3}$.
\end{definition}

A reading is \emph{flagged} (surfaced as an advisory suggestion) when
$\Ft \ge 0.9$ and the value exceeds the prior median, or, for two-sided metrics,
when $|\Ft - \tfrac12| \ge 0.45$. The median guard prevents flagging a value
that is high only because the codebase is unusually clean. Every reading also
reports the global percentile $\Flang(x)$ beside $\Ft(x)$, so that a codebase
that is uniformly atypical relative to the language remains visible: relative
percentiles alone would call its median file typical.

\begin{remark}[Ties at the best value]
Percentiles are reported on the mid-rank scale, which gives a tie block of mass
$p_0$ at the best value the percentile $p_0/2$. Using that scale for quality
would cap a perfect file at $a = 1 - p_0/2$ whenever most of its codebase is
also perfect, which is common: 46--88\% of files have zero divergence and
52--63\% zero fan metrics. Quality therefore uses the strict lower CDF, under
which the best observed value has $\Ft^{<} = 0$ and $a = 1$, and a tie block
shares the quality of its best member. Mid-rank remains the reported percentile
and drives flags, where tie symmetry matters.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Where the population pass runs.} Project scans rescore
advisories against the scanned files: the CLI command \texttt{topos evaluate -r}
and the MCP tool \texttt{topos\_evaluate\_project}. Single-file calls have $n = 0$ and use the
prior only. PR recap deliberately keeps prior-only scores so before/after
deltas compare like with like.

\section{Banded pillar score}

\begin{definition}
With $\wA = 1/3$, the pillar score is
\[
S(G, A) =
\begin{cases}
\tau + (G - \tau)\,A^{\wA}, & G \ge \tau,\\
G\,A^{\wA}, & G < \tau,
\end{cases}
\qquad G \in [0,1],\ A \in [\varepsilon, 1].
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:band}
For all $G \in [0,1]$ and $A \in [\varepsilon, 1]$:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Identity:} $S(G, 1) = G$.
  \item \emph{No crossing:} $S(G, A) \ge \tau \iff G \ge \tau$. Hence, with
        Corollary~\ref{cor:gate}, a pillar passes iff $100\,S \ge 50$.
  \item \emph{Monotonicity:} $S$ is nondecreasing in $A$ for fixed $G$, and
        nondecreasing in $G$ for fixed $A$ (strictly increasing, since
        $A^{\wA} > 0$).
  \item \emph{Bounded advisory influence:} $S \le G$; if $G \ge \tau$ then
        $S \in [\tau + (G - \tau)\varepsilon^{\wA},\, G]$, and if $G < \tau$ then
        $S \in [G \varepsilon^{\wA},\, G]$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $\lambda = A^{\wA} \in [\varepsilon^{\wA}, 1]$; $\lambda$ is
nondecreasing in $A$ and $\lambda = 1$ iff $A = 1$.

(1) Both branches reduce to $G$ when $\lambda = 1$.

(2) If $G \ge \tau$, then $S = \tau + (G - \tau)\lambda \ge \tau$ because
$G - \tau \ge 0$ and $\lambda > 0$. If $G < \tau$, then
$S = G\lambda \le G < \tau$.

(3) In $A$: on the upper branch $S$ is $\tau$ plus a nonnegative multiple of
$\lambda$; on the lower branch it is a nonnegative multiple of $\lambda$.
In $G$: each branch is affine in $G$ with slope $\lambda > 0$. At the junction,
$\lim_{G \to \tau^-} S = \tau\lambda \le \tau = S(\tau, A)$, so the jump at
$G = \tau$ is upward and $S$ is strictly increasing on all of $[0,1]$.

(4) $\lambda \le 1$ gives $S \le G$ on both branches (upper:
$\tau + (G-\tau)\lambda \le \tau + (G - \tau)$). The lower bounds follow from
$\lambda \ge \varepsilon^{\wA}$.
\end{proof}

With $\varepsilon = 10^{-3}$ and $\wA = 1/3$, $\varepsilon^{\wA} = 0.1$: advisories
can remove at most $90\%$ of a passing pillar's margin above $\tau$, and never
the pass itself. When $A < 1$, $S$ is discontinuous at $G = \tau$; this is the
intended veto. The construction is the veto-plus-concordance pattern of ELECTRE
methods~\cite{roy1991}: gates hold a veto, advisories only rank within the side
the veto chose.

\section{The continuous lattice}\label{sec:lattice}

Let $L = [0,1]$ with $x \wedge y = \min(x,y)$, $x \vee y = \max(x,y)$ and Gödel
implication $x \to y = 1$ if $x \le y$, else $y$.

\begin{proposition}
$L^{\mathcal P} = [0,1]^4$ with componentwise operations is a Gödel algebra,
i.e.\ a prelinear Heyting algebra.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$L$ is a bounded chain. In any bounded chain, $z \le (x \to y) \iff
z \wedge x \le y$: if $x \le y$ both sides hold for all $z$; if $x > y$, then
$z \wedge x \le y$ iff $z \le y$ (because $\min(z,x) \le y < x$ forces $z \le y$,
and conversely), and $x \to y = y$. So $L$ is a Heyting algebra, and it is
prelinear: $(x \to y) \vee (y \to x) = 1$ since one of $x \le y$, $y \le x$
holds. Heyting algebras are defined by equations (the residuation law can be
stated equationally), and prelinearity is an equation, so both are preserved
by direct products with componentwise operations: the residuation law holds in
each coordinate, and $(s \to t) \vee (t \to s)$ is $1$ in every coordinate.
Prelinear Heyting algebras are exactly Gödel algebras~\cite{hajek1998}.
\end{proof}

Write $\Omega = \{0,1\}^{\mathcal P}$ for the 16-element verdict lattice and
define the $\tau$-cut $c : [0,1]^4 \to \Omega$ by $c(s)_k = \ind{s_k \ge \tau}$.
By Proposition~\ref{prop:band}, $c(S) = (p_k)_k$ is exactly the verdict vector.

\begin{proposition}
$c$ is a bounded-lattice homomorphism: $c(s \wedge t) = c(s) \wedge c(t)$,
$c(s \vee t) = c(s) \vee c(t)$, $c(\mathbf 0) = \mathbf 0$, $c(\mathbf 1) = \mathbf 1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Coordinatewise, $\ind{\min(x,y) \ge \tau} = \ind{x \ge \tau} \wedge
\ind{y \ge \tau}$ and $\ind{\max(x,y) \ge \tau} = \ind{x \ge \tau} \vee
\ind{y \ge \tau}$; the constants are immediate since $0 < \tau \le 1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}
$c$ does not preserve implication or negation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take one coordinate with $0 < y < x < \tau$. Then $x \to y = y < \tau$, so
$c(x \to y) = 0$, whereas $c(x) \to c(y) = 0 \to 0 = 1$. For negation
$\neg x = x \to 0$: if $0 < x < \tau$ then $\neg x = 0$ and $c(\neg x) = 0$, but
$\neg c(x) = \neg 0 = 1$.
\end{proof}

Consequently, implication and negation are evaluated on the crisp generators
$p_k$, never on the continuous scores.

\paragraph{Medal scores.} Any verdict defined by a lattice polynomial in the
$p_k$ (meets and joins only) has a continuous score given by the same
polynomial in the $S_k$, and the cut of that score is the verdict. For a set of
pillars $M$, the score of ``all of $M$'' is $\min_{k \in M} S_k$. The medal tiers
count pillars: ``at least $j$ of $4$'' is $\bigvee_{|M| = j} \bigwedge_{k \in M}
p_k$, whose continuous score is the $j$-th largest $S_k$. So the PLATINUM score
is $\min_k S_k$, and a file is GOLD or better iff its third-largest pillar score
is at least $50$.

\section{SECURE advisory reporting}

The SECURE gate keeps exactly two inputs, $\texttt{cpg.dangerous\_calls}$ and
$\texttt{cpg.taint\_flows}$, both zero-tolerance. When the embedded Sighthound
engine scans a file (Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, Go), its findings carry
severity, confidence, CWE, and title, which are kept on each finding and
summarized in a report-only block beside the pillar:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{max\_severity} $\in$ \{none, low, medium, high, critical\};
\item \texttt{actionable}: findings with severity high or critical and
  confidence not low;
\item \texttt{taint}: taint-mode findings;
\item \texttt{omitted}: allowlisted findings beyond the display cap of 20;
\item \texttt{by\_family}: a histogram over the closed set \{injection, xss,
  deserialization, path, crypto, auth, other\}, mapped by CWE then by tags.
\end{itemize}
Counts cover every allowlisted finding before the cap, and findings are shown
in order of severity, confidence, mode (taint first), and line.

These quantities deliberately do \emph{not} enter $A_{\textsc{secure}}$, $G$, or
$S$. Sighthound runs only when the SECURE gate has already failed, or on an
explicit opt-in (\texttt{security\_scan} on single-file evaluation). Folding
its output into the score would make $S$ depend on whether a scan ran, breaking
the comparability of scores across files. The codebase-relative construction of Section~\ref{sec:advisory} also does
not apply: the readings
are absolute severities on closed ordinal scales, not distributions over a
codebase. An absent block means the scanner did not run, which is distinct
from a scan that returned no findings.

\paragraph{Allowlist and grade cap.} Allowlisted findings are partitioned into
acknowledged and active over the \emph{complete} finding list; only the
displayed active list is cut to 20. The grade cap (an acknowledged risk may not
buy the top element: if the allowlist makes a file IDEAL, the SECURE generator
is removed from its verdict) fires only when the acknowledged set is non-empty.
Earlier, the cap on displayed findings ran before the partition, so a file with
more than 20 scanner findings whose only acknowledged finding fell past position
20 had an empty acknowledged set and kept an allowlist-bought IDEAL. The change
corrects that case and is the only verdict change in this release. It requires
a raw SECURE failure fully covered by the allowlist, every other generator
satisfied, more than 20 scanner findings (mostly Sighthound rules the CPG gate
does not count), and the acknowledged finding ordered after the twentieth.

\section{Limitations and open questions}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{$\wA$ is a judgment call.} It sets how far advisories may move a
        score within its band. Proposition~\ref{prop:band} bounds the effect but
        does not choose the value.
  \item \emph{NAVIGABLE and SIMPLE overlap.} With $95\%$ of NAVIGABLE failures
        also failing a SIMPLE gate, the generators are not empirically
        independent on this corpus. Whether they should remain separate
        generators is out of scope here.
  \item \emph{Single-file calls are prior-only.} Without a population,
        advisories are read against the language prior, so a file's score can
        differ between a single-file call and a project scan. PR recap uses the
        prior for comparability.
  \item \emph{Percentiles measure typicality, not defectiveness.} Being in the
        tail of top open-source code is evidence of atypicality; thresholds
        derived from distributions do not by themselves predict
        faults~\cite{lavazza2016,elemam2001}. Advisory messages say ``atypical
        vs.\ top OSS'', never ``buggy''. Software metric distributions are
        heavy-tailed~\cite{concas2007,ferreira2012}, which is why we work with
        ranks and $\log(1 + x)$ rather than raw means.
  \item \emph{Aggregation choices elsewhere.} Quality-model aggregation remains
        an active area~\cite{mordal2013,wagner2015}; our only claim is that the
        gate layer must use $\min$ to agree with the verdict.
  \item \emph{Prior corpus.} The prior table inherits the leaderboard's
        selection (popular open-source packages) and its version mix.
        Threshold derivation from benchmarks of this kind is discussed
        in~\cite{alves2010,ernst2018,mori2018}.
\end{itemize}
